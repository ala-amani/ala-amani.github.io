Publications

LLM-Assisted Human-in-the-Loop Validation for Explainable Network Intrusion Alerts

A. Amani Tehrani, A. Horri, L. Samimi-Dehkordi · Under review, 12th International Symposium on Telecommunications (IST 2026)

A validation-first workflow: signed SHAP evidence is given to a constrained local LLM as a review aid and then shown to a human analyst, who decides whether an alert is correct, a false positive or the wrong attack class. Only analyst-confirmed labels are stored for controlled retraining. The full path was tested in a four-scenario synthetic network.

Human-in-the-Loop Validation for Explainable Network Intrusion Detection

A. Amani Tehrani, A. Horri, L. Samimi-Dehkordi · Submitted, Journal of Computer Virology and Hacking Techniques (Springer)

Extends a published explainable-NIDS pipeline (seven classifiers, three datasets) with an alert-level reliability estimate, constrained LLM commentary, three-way analyst adjudication and gated retraining on an untouched holdout.

Projects

Explainable intrusion detection with analyst review

Research project, 2025–2026 (first author)

Where the idea came from. Explanations can tell you why a detector raised an alert, but they can't tell you whether the alert is right. I wanted the analyst, not the model, to have the final word, and I wanted the system to learn only from those decisions.

What I built. Everything is written in Python. A Random Forest detector trained on NSL-KDD flags traffic, SHAP explains each alert, and a reliability score helps decide which alerts to look at first. A local LLM (qwen3:4b, running through Ollama) comments on the evidence but has to stick to a fixed format. The analyst then marks each alert as correct, a false positive or the wrong attack type, and has to give a reason. Those reviewed labels go into a separate store and are only used to train a candidate model, which must be evaluated on its own before it can replace anything. To test the whole path end to end, I also built a small reproducible network with a router, an access point, a laptop and a phone, and ran four scenarios through it.

Who I worked with. I did this under the supervision of Dr. Abbas Horri and Dr. Leila Samimi-Dehkordi, and it became the two manuscripts above. I'm happy to share the code and manuscripts once the review process allows.
